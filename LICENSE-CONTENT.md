This file explains the split, because "open source" does not automatically mean
"the writing is yours to reuse".

- the articles and screenshots under `docs/`

## What that means in practice

**You may** read it, quote it with attribution and a link, and link to the
original.

**You may not** republish it as your own, sell it, or publish a modified version
of it — including translations published as if they were the original.

## Why the split

The code exists to be read and learned from; that is the point of publishing it,
and MIT is the least friction for that. The content is a personal body of
writing. Licensing it the same way would permit it to be lifted wholesale into
someone else's commercial site, which is not what publishing a repository is
meant to agree to.

## If you want to use the content

Attribution is not the only condition, so please ask first. Open an issue or use
the contact details on the site. Requests to translate an article, or to reuse
one in teaching material, are usually welcome — the licence is restrictive in
order to make that conversation happen rather than to refuse it.
